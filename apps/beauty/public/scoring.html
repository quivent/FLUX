Architecture · proposal

Scoring

No judge's number in the loop. Every new frame is compared with the collection's best, and the difference is scored aspect by aspect, better (+), worse (−) or the same, measured as well as judged. Evolution acts on that profile. A single number is only for judging one image on its own.

JuryControls
Why today's scores don't move
  • A six-word ladder. Each juror answers one word per axis: failed, broken, obvious, competent, specialist, flawless. Each word maps to a fixed number, so a frame can only land on a few scores. Qwen gives 78.0 to almost every frame, Pixtral 90.5 to most, and different frames tie.
  • A curve that saturates. The published score is a percentile against recent history. In a young collection it reads 99.9 "masterpiece" on frame after frame.
  • A uniqueness penalty that fights the goal. It subtracts for resembling recent frames, and a collection on one subject is supposed to resemble itself.
  • Seed noise. Every render uses a random seed, so two frames differ mostly by seed, not by the prompt change being tested. Evolution can't tell cause from chance.

1 · Objective

Scoring the diff

Measured, not judged. No language model involved. Both models are already on the box.

MeasureFromAnswers
Δ previousDINOv2-giant, cosine distance to the previous frameDid the prompt edit actually change the image? Near zero means the edit didn't take.
Δ championDINOv2-giant, distance to the championHow far this challenger is from the best frame: a small step or a leap.
Subject holdSigLIP, image-to-text similarity with the fixed subjectIs it still the same subject? A drop is subject drift, whatever the jurors think of the frame.
Direction holdSigLIP, similarity with each of the teacher's directionsIs the teacher's direction still visible in the frame?

The diff is scored twice: measured here, and named by the jurors in section 2. The two together tell evolution whether its edit caused the change.

2 · Subjective

The jurors score the diff, aspect by aspect

  • Each vision juror gets the champion and the challenger in one request, labeled A and B, and scores its own aspects.
  • For each aspect it answers with a sign and a size: +1 / +2 / +3 if B is better (slightly, clearly, decisively), 0 if the same, −1 / −2 / −3 if worse. It adds one sentence naming the visible difference.
  • It's asked twice with A and B swapped. Where the two answers disagree, that aspect is 0. This cancels the bias toward whichever image comes first.
  • Influence on /control weights each juror's aspects when two jurors score the same aspect. Influence 0 means not asked.
  • No absolute number is produced in the loop. The six-word ladder is kept only as a floor (broken or failed is out) and for judging a single image on its own.

3 · Evolution

Acting on the profile

Each edit aims at one aspect, the one the jurors named weakest, and says so. Its profile is then read against that aim:

Aimed-at aspectOther aspectsMeasured diffReadingNext step
+none worse than −1anyThe edit workedNew champion; aim at the next weakest aspect
+something −2 or worseanyA trade: gained one aspect, lost anotherKeep the champion; retry the aim with a narrower edit
0—smallThe edit didn't takeA bolder edit on the same aspect
0 or −—clearThe image changed, just not for the betterKeep the champion; try a different approach to that aspect
any—subject hold fallsDriftRejected, whatever the jurors say

The collection's record is the chain of accepted profiles: which aspects moved, when, and by which edit. That is its history of improvement, with no number standing in for it.

The prompt

Subject (fixed) + craft phrase (the only part that evolves, at most 40 words: lighting, color, composition and pose, cut and construction, fabric, finish, camera) + the teacher's directions (attached verbatim at render time, so changing them on /control applies at once). The model that proposes edits never restates the directions, so it can't drop or soften them, and the prompt can't bloat.

Each prompt and seed is rendered once; while the next edit is being made, the wall keeps the GPU busy. If no usable edit comes back after one retry, the champion's prompt is re-rendered with a fresh seed as a new baseline, so the loop never stalls.

What it takes
  • Pixtral and Qwen restarted with 2 images per request (--limit-mm-per-prompt image:2). About 1 minute each.
  • DINOv2-giant and SigLIP loaded once in the jury service: about 4 GB of GPU memory, which fits.
  • Paired seeds: the filler renders each challenger with its champion's seed.
  • Cost: two requests per juror per frame (the order swap), about twice today's jury time. At 7 seconds a verdict, that's about 15.
  • No change to the Go server or the gallery. Receipts gain the pairwise and diff fields; the jury page's live feed shows them.
Your decisions
  1. Paired seeds. The same seed for champion and challenger (clean diffs, less variety), or two seeds per challenger (more robust, twice the renders)?
  2. What counts as an accepted step. Proposed: the aimed-at aspect +1 or better, and no other aspect below −1.
  3. The aspects. The proposed list above, or the ones that matter for these shoots.
  4. Objective against subjective. Should measured subject hold be a hard gate (as proposed), or only a signal?
  5. The uniqueness penalty. Drop it inside a collection (proposed), or keep a small version to stop the collection repeating itself?
